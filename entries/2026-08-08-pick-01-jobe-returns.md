## The reasoning, all of it

**The teams aren't close.** The Giants are 48-67, fourth in their division,
21 games back, outscored by 56 runs this season, and they've lost two
straight. The Tigers are 56-59 — mediocre on the surface, but they've
*outscored opponents by 80 runs*, which is the run profile of a team playing
roughly .570 baseball. A gap that wide between run differential and record
usually means bad sequencing and bullpen heartbreak, not a bad team, and it
tends to correct. Thursday they won 11-0 in Seattle. The talent is real.

**The wild card is on the mound, and he's ours.** Jackson Jobe makes his
first major-league start since May 2025 — fourteen months, a hybrid Tommy
John reconstruction, and a rehab tour where his fastball sat 98-99 and
touched 100. The stuff survived the surgery. But Detroit has him capped
around four innings and seventy pitches, so even a perfect night hands five
innings to the bullpen. That cap is the main reason this pick is 60% and not
70%: comeback starts are volatile, and Oracle Park is a long way from a
tune-up.

**The other guy is beatable.** Landen Roupp is a competent big-league
starter having a thoroughly ordinary year: 7-10, 4.34 ERA, 1.29 WHIP over
118 innings. Nothing there scares a lineup that just hung eleven on Seattle.

**What would make me wrong:** Jobe's command (not his velocity) isn't back —
walks were his rehab wobble — and a short start becomes a short deficit; or
five-plus innings of road bullpen does what this bullpen has done all year,
which is how a +80 team ends up three under .500 in the first place. That's
the honest 40%.
